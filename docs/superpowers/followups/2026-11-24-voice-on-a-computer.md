# La voix sur un ordinateur (chantier D) — ce qui a été décidé en ton nom

Ce document garde, en un seul endroit, tout ce qui a été décidé à ta place pendant la construction
de la voix (plan `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-24-voice-on-a-computer.md`, conception
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-24-la-voix-sur-un-ordinateur-design.md`). Tes propres mots sont
dans la partie 2 de la conception ; tout le reste a été choisi pour toi, par l'auteur de la
conception, par le contrôleur ou par ceux qui ont construit.

Comment le lire : chaque ligne du tableau dit ce qui a été décidé, et ce que ça te coûte si c'est
faux. Les lignes sont rangées par sujet : ce qui est entendu et où ça va ; qui parle ; ordinaire et
sensible ; la phrase secrète ; une autre personne ou un inconnu devant une page ; ce qui n'est pas
pour lui ; les empreintes de voix ; le service de parole ; le programme de texte ; les essais et la
façon de travailler. Le texte technique reste en anglais, comme dans les registres précédents, et
il a été vérifié dans le code tel qu'il est aujourd'hui : quand la construction a changé une
décision du départ, c'est ce qui a été construit qui est écrit, et la ligne dit ce qu'elle
remplace.

À la fin, ta réponse aux douze cas de l'audit « le code contre le juge », avec ce que chacun coûte
si le jugement se trompe, puis deux listes courtes : les choix qui restent à toi (« OPEN CHOICES FOR
THE OWNER ») et les limites connues que la construction a acceptées (« KNOWN LIMITS »).

## The decisions

| # | Ruling | Cost if wrong |
|---|---|---|
| | **Hearing and where it goes** | |
| H1 | What is heard (the sound, the voice print, the quick judgement `attention`, the sorting `heard_sorting`: `llm.tasks.HOME_ONLY`) goes only to a model at home unless the owner sets `speech.hear_outside` to yes: ONE setting for the sound and for the text of what is not said to him (decisions 3 and 13). Saying goes wherever a model that says is found. A sentence that may be a phrase (a phrase moment lives, or a proof window is open at that microphone) is heard at home whatever the setting; where no model at home can hear it, it is dropped. | an owner who would let only one of these go out has no finer setting; a house with no hearing model at home is deaf at a microphone while a window is open there |
| H2 | "At home" is the gatekeeper's word: the provider's address, resolved by the gatekeeper, is in zone house or host (a read-only internal route, cached, fails closed; no second list in the core). The stack's own speech service (`HOME_SPEECH_ID`, at `DOMO_SPEECH_URL`) is at home BY DECLARATION, since the zones call the assistant's own networks "never"; changing its address away from the declared one loses it. Replaces an earlier ruling on `host.docker.internal`. | one more internal call per provider (cached); an owner who points `DOMO_SPEECH_URL` at a machine he does not control sends hearing there (his own setting) |
| H3 | `HOME_SPEECH_ID` is fixed and hashed (from "home-speech-service"), so a changed address is the same key; `_the_key` looks it up first and matches by address only a keyless key the owner gave. A name the gatekeeper cannot resolve answers zone `never` with a 200. | a typo in `DOMO_SPEECH_URL` looks like a refused address, found in the gatekeeper's log |
| H4 | A key that lists no model able to think (speech only: hear, say, voice print) never becomes the household's general key, decided from what the key lists, never from a provider's name (merge with master's R65). | an owner whose only key is speech-only has no general key until he gives a thinking one |
| H5 | Sound answers are relayed byte for byte but watched (detect-only): a secret found whole cuts the stream before any of it is sent on; only 2xx answers count as sound; headers and text answers keep the full scrub; in sound only secrets of 8 bytes or more are looked for. Replaces R3 and design choice 6 ("the scrub does not touch sound") toward safer. | a short code echoed inside sound passes; a sound that holds a key's bytes by chance is cut (negligible) |
| H6 | Calls of sound are metered like chat calls (reserve, firm limits, settle; units: seconds heard, characters said); the five new task kinds are ordinary, not `PROTECTED`; a free home call still reserves at 0 and is refused when the home key is over its limit. | hearing refused on a home key the owner limited |
| H7 | A microphone is an opaque id the page makes (random per browser profile, plus the chosen input): the page's claim, never a proof of place (R5). | clearing the site's data makes a "new microphone", learnt again |
| H8 | The socket checks itself before accepting: exactly one `Origin` (no `Referer` stands in), the session cookie; `hello` carries the person the page was rendered for (a mismatch closes, the page reloads); the session is read again before each utterance; the server's frame limit sits just above the route's; the edge has an uncapped `/chat/voice` handle for WebSocket requests only (over HTTP/2 the socket's bytes are a request body). | an uncapped upload path for signed-in sockets, bounded by the route's own limits |
| H9 | The page's speech detector is Silero VAD v6.2 on ONNX Runtime Web 1.31.1 (MIT, vendored, one thread, no worker): the page's policy gains only `'wasm-unsafe-eval'`; windows of 512 samples (32 ms); the page's answers carry `Permissions-Policy: microphone=(self)`. | swapping the detector means three vendored files and `detector.js`; the microphone cannot be used in a frame of another origin |
| H10 | A hidden tab keeps listening (the person turned the voice on for the page; the indicator stays true); everything stops on `pagehide`. | one line to pause on `visibilitychange` |
| H11 | The language heard travels on `TurnRequest.language`; the turn reads it, then the person's language, then the house's (design choice 1; was left out of Part 1). | a person who speaks another language than the one they read is written to in the spoken one for that turn; no per-person setting |
| H12 | A card is said by the words-only rendering the API channel already has; what a card shows once is never said aloud (it stays on the page's card); a key is never said; fenced code is not read aloud; a capital letter before a full stop is an initial only before a capitalised word. Reading a once-shown thing aloud on request (design 6.8) is not built. | a person who asks to hear a code must read it on the page |
| H13 | A reply's end is bounded by a stall (`STALL_SECONDS`: no sentence finished and no chunk sent), plus a ceiling per sentence (20 s + 0.3 s per character), never by a fixed total. | a very slow link or voice cuts long sentences; a cold voice's first sentence may meet the ceiling |
| H14 | Words said while a reply is written are kept and answered, never refused; words kept while ANOTHER channel's reply is written join that turn (`TurnRequest.also_hears`) and its answer is said aloud at the page that heard them; words given up and failures are told to the page by typed messages put in his words. | the part of that other reply written before the turn read the words is not said aloud |
| H15 | Nothing `depends_on` the speech service and it has no start order: the household works without it while it prepares. | the first sentences of the first minutes are not heard |
| H16 | Left to other chantiers (design choice 13): hearing a friend in a headset, a proof means made by a tool, microphones of the rooms, the phone, the computer's own application. | none here |
| | **Who speaks** | |
| W1 | Three states, computed by the code from its clues: sure, unsure, unknown; every voice clue is weak (a voice alone never establishes anybody); the rights are always those of the probable person, never the page's by principle (design 6.5). | none if the clues are right |
| W2 | The voice clue (ruling A19, which replaces the T9 wording "far from everybody known"): degree 0.60 or more reads "for"; 0.30 to 0.60 is unsure; under 0.30 with the voice learnt reads "nobody known" when no known other reads "for", and "another person" when a known other reads "for" and is nearer than the page's person. | a cold taken for a stranger, or a stranger for the page's person (measured in the trials, X8; at one's own page see audit case 5) |
| W3 | The numbers (replaces R12): `FOR_AT` 0.60, `AGAINST_AT` 0.30, a sound under 1.5 s is short and capped at 0.40, utterances within 30 s join; learning needs 3 like utterances on 2 visits an hour apart; the base moves 2% at most once a visit; a passing print lives 3 days; a voice nobody knows is forgotten 30 days after it was last heard; the similarity bounds of each print model (their values: X8) live in the speech service's data and reach the core through `/v1/models`, no copy in the core. | too strict: he is slow to know you; too loose: a near voice passes; to measure on real voices |
| W4 | Intonation (the owner: "l'intonation et la manière de parler") moves a reading by at most 0.10 and only inside its band, never across a threshold, never in what is learnt. | intonation weighs little in the code's degree; the manner reaches the judge through his own clue |
| W5 | A short sound, or one with no usable print, not joined to a recent print that reads "for", is the page's person and sure for what is ordinary, but unsure for what is sensitive (`voice_too_short`), even with a kept proof; it is never "against" nor "an unknown voice" (ruling T22). | a short "oui" after a silence to something sensitive costs one judge call; a stranger's short "yes" reads unsure rather than unknown |
| W6 | A tie with a known other who reads "for" reads unsure and names the other. | twins are never "for" by voice alone at each other's pages |
| W7 | A reading held up by a passing print (a cold) is never "for": unsure, with a marker; it keeps the person from being taken for a stranger. | a person with a cold is unsure for the days of the cold (sensitive things go to the judge) |
| W8 | The standing travels on `Actor` (`standing`, `voice_too_short`, `page_person_id`), never as a separate `decide` parameter. | a later caller writes `replace(actor, ...)` |
| W9 | The judge's speaker section was rewritten in place (7 996 of 8 000 characters, the cap untouched): microphone, degree, "unknown", source `domo`; disturb little; a less sure speaker is no reason to ask for an ordinary action. | the judge reads less nuance (no evaluation with a real model was run) |
| W10 | His reading of how someone speaks (`speaker_manner_note`, `voice.manner_note`) is a weak clue of his: silent, ordinary, never changes the standing, kept beside a voice that reads against, refused after outside text. | none on the standing; the judge reads one more weak clue |
| W11 | The "nobody" row carries no session clue and none of the page person's kept clues. | the judge sees no session for nobody (the barrier refuses nobody first anyway) |
| W12 | What an unsure person may hear is his judgement (design choice 5 bis), but the code also leaves out every memory marked private when it is not sure, and for a too-short utterance; the conversation stays in his context, and the line on who speaks tells him not to say private things aloud. | a hoarse person gets poorer answers, with no word why, until he is sure again (a proof); kept in code by the owner (audit case 8) |
| W13 | A proof stays a clue 15 minutes for that person, channel and microphone, extended while they go on speaking, and only while the voice does not contradict them; a stranger or another person talking never keeps it alive. | none expected |
| W14 | What he says on his own is said aloud at a page only to the person whose voice was heard last there and does not read against them (the page's person until another voice is heard); "the last voice" lasts 180 s. | news turns for the page's person wait while a stranger was heard last |
| | **Ordinary and sensitive** | |
| O1 | "Do" by default for what is ordinary when he is not sure who speaks, with the rights of the probable person; the owner may say "judge" (`voice.ordinary_when_unsure`; design choice 11). Acting in the house is ordinary only when declared harmless (the owner, 2026-11-27: "the physical that carries risks is judged, like unlocking the door; physical actions with no risk may pass with less certainty": X35). | an imitator at a signed-in page gets ordinary things done, a harmless act in the house included (a light, never a lock) |
| O2 | Ordinary is a short list written by hand (`policy.catalog.WEAKLY_FINE`: talking, renaming oneself or him, his texts and writing, his models, keeping a memory, nicknames, the household's members and rights, learning notes, the mark of his own idea, stopping the listening, the manner note); everything else is sensitive, an unknown type included. `plan.set` moved to sensitive (the design had "a reminder for oneself" as ordinary). | a reminder set by a short utterance costs one judge call; see OPEN CHOICES for what an unsure speaker can still do |
| O3 | Acting physically alone (`physical`) is ordinary only when the operation also declares `"harmless": true` (X35); not declared, it is risky and judged under doubt. Replaces "acting physically alone is ordinary, as the approved design says". | a tool he wrongly declared harmless is done for an unsure voice; every tool made before is judged under doubt until rebuilt |
| O4 | Under doubt, a launch of a tool he made is ordinary only when its operations declare nothing but acting in the house declared harmless (X35) AND its means cannot do more (no declared account: any account counts as "may write"; no reach outside the house); otherwise the judge is asked up front. A nested launch inherits the doubt; a doubted run may not time a launch for later (kept in code: the program asks it while running, with nobody to judge it then, and the doubt cannot be carried to the later launch, which would run as someone the code is sure of; the model's own `tool_launch_when` in a doubted turn is judged as usual); an `out` naming an account its operation did not declare is refused (`needs_agreement`, nothing sent). Sure runs are unchanged. | a light through a hub with a stored account costs a judge call (a proof when the judge is down); a tool that chains to a sensitive one stops half-way |
| O5 | A launch is judged on who asks with the union of the risks its operations declare (harmless only when every operation that acts in the house is declared so); its program's requests are not judged on identity again. | a tool with one paying operation and one light, or with a light and a lock, is judged even for the light |
| O6 | A judge silent, failing or with no model, on a sensitive thing from someone the code is not sure of, asks a proof (not master's `do`/`yes` fallback); his own idea keeps master's stricter rule (nothing done). Rewrites R13. | while the judge's model is down, an unsure speaker is asked a proof for every sensitive thing |
| O7 | An agreement in words from someone the code is not sure of, to a sensitive request, is weighed by the judge; when he asks more, the request is raised (`asked_by` `judge`) and shown again. What it asked before the raise is kept (`ask_before_doubt`, migration 0067): a later yes the code is sure of is held to that and to the rules of now, not to the raise (audit case 10); a proof the judge asks at creation because the requester's voice was doubted is lowered the same way. A call carrying a stop's yes needs no hook of its own. The doubted yes is now really weighed by the judge (X33, `Turn._weighing`): before, the judge had no model at the moment the answer was read, so every unsure yes asked a proof; now his `do` lets it pass with no proof. Sure speakers and the typed channel are unchanged. | a sure yes that is not the person's (a page left open) is held to the old ask; a judge who asked a proof for the action itself while the requester was doubted is lowered too; a chain step and the cost stop are not covered; an unsure yes the judge wrongly takes for the person's does a sensitive thing with no proof |
| | **The spoken phrase and the proof window** | |
| P1 | The heard text of the phrase crosses the core in memory only, on its way to the gatekeeper, like a typed password (design 6.9 amended, R1). | a memory dump of the core at that instant holds it; mending = the gatekeeper transcribes itself |
| P2 | The phrase is kept in a table of its own in the vault's file, not as a vault secret (R2); a first saying waits 5 minutes (`pending_at`), sealed with its own associated data. | a second storage path to review |
| P3 | The gatekeeper compares with tolerance (design choice 9): `it_is` from 0.85, `almost` from 0.60, else `nothing_like`; a phrase has at least 3 words and 12 characters. | too tolerant, a near phrase passes; too strict, you say it again |
| P4 | Caps per phrase: 5 near misses, and 50 comparisons of any kind, in 15 minutes (never cleared by `it_is`); a held phrase answers `held_back` to anything; an unknown reference answers `nothing_like` with a 200 (never an error telling whether a phrase exists). | a person who talks a lot while a proof waits is held back sooner |
| P5 | Agreeing, changing or removing one's own phrase: the person themself; a speaker the code is not sure of is weighed by the judge with the clues (audit case 2); agreeing or changing it asks a proof by another means first when they have one, even from a sure speaker (audit case 3: kept in code by the owner); in a reserved moment (a row, the next two sayings) bound to one microphone; the weakness (the room can hear it) said once and remembered; agreeing again always starts afresh; removing it removes it at the gatekeeper too. Agreeing or changing it: nobody else, the owner included (a proof: only its holder sets it). Removing someone else's: the owner, or an adult for a child, may ask it, always judged, and only the judge's verdict removes it (a silent judge removes nothing and asks nothing: X32); anyone else is refused. The phrase is never an idea of his. | an unsure voice that is not the person may remove the person's phrase when the judge is wrong (a silent judge asks a proof of the phrase's holder); a sure person with a password must give it before agreeing a phrase; see X32 for someone else's |
| P6 | THE PROOF WINDOW (Task 24, round 5; replaces Task 14's "proof moment" of 2 sentences / 60 s, and round 3's "every sentence while a request waits"): open at a microphone for person P while P has a request asking a proof and an agreed phrase (held back or not) AND a reply to P was said there in the last 90 s (`PROOF_WINDOW_S`, from the end of that reply). While open, EVERY sentence of that microphone, whoever it is taken for, is heard at home and compared by code with the phrase of each open window before the quick judgement, any buffer, discussion or model. Outside any window nothing is compared. | see KNOWN LIMITS (a phrase said elsewhere is not protected) |
| P7 | Inside a window: it holds and the voice is P's: the proof is given, nothing written; it holds from a voice not surely P's (overheard, nobody, too short): checked by code, then the judge decides with the clues (`another_proof`), on the person's key (design choice 5 ter); a near miss: dropped, P told it did not work; held back: dropped, P told once per hold and window with the same words (a bystander cannot tell held from wrong); the gatekeeper out of reach: dropped, one wake per outage and microphone; nothing like it: the sentence goes its usual way. A phrase that holds is given to each request waiting for it, newest first. | one judge call per doubted proof; a few comparisons more |
| P8 | Another known person's sentence at a page whose person awaits a proof is not dropped: it is compared first, as that person's phrase in a voice against them, then goes the other person's usual way. | one more comparison per such sentence |
| P9 | The late-copy rule: a saying taken in a moment or a window is "in hand" at its microphone; a sentence of another microphone that began before it was taken (or within 2 s after) is dropped and that page is told `elsewhere`; a page that closes forgets only its own microphone's marks. | an ordinary sentence said at another page in those seconds is lost |
| P10 | A returning person's means of the phrase is removed with their old access, and the vault forgets its stored copy once the return is committed (final fix wave); a vault that does not answer then keeps nobody out (the copy proves nothing without the means, and agreeing again removes it first). | a sealed copy stays in the vault's file when the vault did not answer at that moment |
| | **Another person, or a stranger, at a page** | |
| S1 | Someone other than the page's person speaks in their own discussion, with their own rights, not shown on this page (design choice 10, R10). | they find their words only on their own page |
| S2 | Heard at a page signed in as another person: a known member is sure there only after a proof given there AND while this sentence's voice reads "for" at degree 0.75 or more (`CLEARLY_AT`: a degree, between `FOR_AT` and the top, not a similarity; about 0.62 of similarity for resnet34's bounds 0.25/0.75), and then acts with their own rights (audit case 6). Otherwise nothing is ordinary (every action judged), decided as themselves and as the page's person, the stricter wins; with the judge silent, an ordinary thing is done without a proof while `voice.ordinary_when_unsure` is `do` (case 7). The context holds what was said and answered at this microphone in this visit; for a sure voice also the notes and plans waiting for them, the assistant judging whether to tell them in that room; for an unsure one only that something waits (case 9); never their earlier conversation, requests not shown here or private memory. Their words never join a turn of theirs running elsewhere: they wait (up to 20 at a page, no time bound: case 11) and get a turn of their own after it; only replies made with that restricted context are said aloud there. | a member whose voice reads 0.60-0.75 there is held to the page person's rules too and judged; with the judge down, someone misread there does an ordinary thing with the stricter rights; a sure voice may hear a private note read aloud in another person's room; their wakes and answers to words typed elsewhere are not said at that page |
| S3 | Replies to another person and to a stranger at a page are said with the page person's speech, metered on them; a stranger's answer is paid on the page person's key (R11). | the page person's key pays for another's chatter (at home: nothing) |
| S4 | A voice nobody knows (design choice 8): one model call per answer, no tool, nothing of anybody in the house. No fixed count stops it any more (the owner, 2026-11-27, "the assistant judges": X34): in that same answer he says whether he goes on and, if not, pauses that voice for the minutes he chooses; while paused, and past the microphone's flood bound, silence, no model at all (the quick judgement and the whose-voice question included); the page person's key keeps its spending limits (no model left: nothing said, `not_now`); one indistinct page notice (`not_now`) for a sentence not taken otherwise, so an imitator learns nothing. Replaces "at most 6 calls per reference AND per microphone in 10 minutes". | he keeps answering a stranger who costs the page person's key up to its limit (up to 360 answers an hour per microphone where no limit is set); a person dropped for another's moment no longer learns why |
| S5 | The word left to the page's person: one per reference and page person, rewritten whole at each answer, left when the exchange ends (10 minutes after its last answer) or at the bound, never two within 10 minutes; decided for him on his own and audited, left only when allowed with no rule asking first; one an owner rule holds is tried again every 10 minutes for 24 h (X14). It is his own note, marked as born of an unknown source (`notes.from_unknown`, migration 0068). The code never refuses the voice tools (stop listening, the phrase, forgetting a voice) for that mark (the owner's rule, 2026-11-26: it all depends on the source, and the judge weighs it like a person would): beside the person's words or in a turn with none (a wake, the note alone, an idea of his fed by it), the call goes to the judge, who reads in `after_outside_words` that the turn holds a note born of an unknown source (a voice nobody knows, or his own note written after reading a program's, a site's or a tool's text). A silent judge asks the person a proof; for an idea of his, he does nothing for now. A note of his left in a turn that read a program's, a site's or a tool's text is marked the same way. Raw text of a program, a site or a tool in the turn beside the person's words or a note is judged too, the judge told which kind (X31); such text alone, with no words of anyone and no note, is refused by code. | lost at a restart; a stranger's relayed word may lead him to stop listening, remove a phrase or forget a voice when the judge is wrong (the person is told; a voice is learnt again, a phrase agreed again); while a marked note waits untold, a voice tool is judged, not done at once |
| S6 | He tells a new voice that he listens when he judges it useful, and remembers it per voice (`warned_at`; design choice 5 quater); he may tell a stranger he does not know their voice. | an imitator learns he failed |
| S7 | A voice nobody knows keeps its first print and is forgotten 30 days after it was last heard; a new one under 1.5 s gets a reference stored nowhere; opening a record for it is left to "Domo crée les personnes" (`channels/voice/stranger.py` says where). | an unknown voice that changes slowly gets a new reference |
| | **What is not said to him** | |
| A1 | The page lowers his sound as soon as someone speaks, mechanically, and raises it again on its own if nothing follows (design choice 4). | a sound that dips for a noise |
| A2 | The quick judgement (task `attention`, at home) says whether a sentence was for him and, while he speaks, `stop`, `resume` or `drop`; no word list in code; a judgement that fails or is late takes the sentence as said to him and he goes on (never silent by reflex). It runs first, then the stranger's path. | a missed "stop" when the small model is weak |
| A3 | A stop or a drop quiets only the microphone that heard it (replaces Task 25's first "every page where his voice is heard"). | with two pages in one room, "stop" near one does not quiet the other |
| A4 | While a phrase moment lives or a window is open at a microphone, no sentence reaches the quick judgement before the code weighed it. | none expected |
| A5 | Not said to him: kept in memory only, by microphone (200 lines, 30 minutes), given in one batch once the room is quiet for 90 s (`heard_sorting`, at home, design choice 5); the sorting may look up and keep; when a line is the page person's own voice heard sure (not too short), it may also change one of that person's own memories (audit case 12); never forget or show; what it keeps is marked as overheard (`others_words`; the origin `unsure_voice` of X17 is the review's, never the sorting's), but `said_by` stays the page's person (a migration); the batch is dropped when the page's socket closes. | a sentence of the person overheard out of context (a quote, a joke) may rewrite one of their memories; nothing overheard from anyone else corrects a memory; who said it is only in the memory's wording; what was said just before a page closed is forgotten |
| A6 | With no model for the quick judgement, the voice stays off and he tells the owner once, offering `speech.hear_outside` or the new setting `voice.after_a_gesture` (hold to talk, `no` by default); with no sorting model he keeps nothing overheard (decision 13). | a page that ignores the state sends free speech, then taken as said to him |
| A7 | A print of a sentence not said to him only keeps the visit, never teaches the voice. | the voice is learnt more slowly |
| A8 | "Stop listening" acts at once (`LiveVoices`): the page saying the reply that asked, else every listening page of the person; no row is kept. Done at once on the person's own words; judged whether it is really their wish when words not theirs are in the turn (a member's note, a note born of an unknown source), also in a turn with no words of theirs (a wake), or when a plan of theirs whose time came keeps their words, or when a program's, a site's or a tool's text is in the turn beside their words or a note ("read me this page, then stop listening": judged, the judge told which kind of text, X31); refused by code only when such text is the only words in the turn (no words of anyone, no note), or with nobody's words at all. A silent judge asks a proof, never a silent stop. | a stop asked while no page listens does not reach a page opened later; a member's or a stranger's relayed word, or a page's text that says "stop listening", may turn a person's microphone off when the judge takes it for their wish (they turn it on again) |
| | **Voice prints and forgetting** | |
| V1 | A voice is learnt only when something else says who speaks (the page, a proof), from 3 like utterances on 2 visits (design choice 7); `learn` decides on the single print it stores, never on a joined mean. | slow to know you; or another's voice learnt as yours, undone by "that was not me" |
| V2 | Prints are stored with their model's name; a print of another model is never compared (R8). | changing the print model loses recognition until relearnt |
| V3 | Base and recent prints follow a voice only when it reads "for" against the BASE (raw); the base moves at most once a visit (a visit ends after an hour with nothing heard at that microphone, kept in the database); a cold goes to a passing print that never teaches; a new microphone is entered on the person's base prints of other microphones (worth 0.8) and checked again before it becomes a base. | a fast real change of voice (surgery, puberty) reads unsure until the person asks to learn it again |
| V4 | "The nearest known other" is one thing everywhere (another person's base and recent prints, any microphone, no discount; a passing print names nobody); a voice is not learnt as the person's while a known other reads "for" and is nearer; at an unlearnt person's page another voice blocks only at "for". | a relative close to a known person is learnt later; a known person with a never-heard cold could be learnt as her over two visits |
| V5 | "That was not me" is the person's own (their own voice only, the speaker is them): it removes the recent and passing prints and the bases learnt since; from a speaker the code is not sure of, any erase is weighed by the judge (audit case 2). | cannot undo small mixes already made into an older base (see OPEN CHOICES); an unsure voice that is not the person may erase the learnt voice when the judge is wrong (it is learnt again) |
